Publication standard

What a published listing will carry

Every listing published on this page will carry the same eight verified fields. The standard exists because the common failure of a legal opportunity board is a posting that reads as an offer while omitting the terms that would make it one.

Hiring attorney or firm

The named lawyer or entity responsible for the work, with the jurisdiction where they are admitted.

Compensation

A stated rate or range, the basis for it, and the payment schedule.

Supervision

The person who reviews the work, and the points in the assignment where that review happens.

Jurisdiction

The body of law the work concerns, plus any residency or bar-status requirement.

Deadline

The date applications close, and the expected start window.

Application method

Where materials go, in what format, and what the sender should expect back.

Privacy handling

What happens to an applicant’s materials, who reads them, and how long they are kept.

Last verified

The date a person confirmed the posting still stands.

A posting missing any of the eight stays unpublished.

Available now

Three frameworks are live and free

The workflow lab on the main site carries three frameworks. Each is a plain Markdown file, downloadable and adaptable inside an existing practice, clinic, or course.

Research memorandum

Question, jurisdiction, authority table, synthesis, application, unresolved questions, and supervisor review.

Download Markdown

Case chronology

A source-linked event register carrying disputed-fact, privilege, and completeness states.

Download Markdown

Contract review issue log

A clause inventory and escalation log for routing questions to the supervising attorney.

Download Markdown

They follow the same structure a published assignment would use, so the frameworks stay useful on their own terms.

Where to start meanwhile

Two practical starting points

For attorneys. Adopt the frameworks with a clerk, extern, or associate already working under your supervision. The research memorandum is the usual entry point, because it surfaces the authority trail and the open questions before a draft arrives. The review gates are written into the framework itself, so the points where your judgment is required stay visible on the page.

For law students. Work the same three frameworks against a closed matter, a clinic file, or a class problem. The structure is the transferable part, and it reads the same way whoever assigns the work. A completed chronology or issue log also shows a supervising attorney how you organize a problem.
